Hi everyone,

The three month trip of 2019 started a week or so ago. The first stop was where I am now, in Malaysia...Kuala Lumpur.

Nothing much seems to have changed since I was here in September last year except that big underground-station works that had been going on for years in the area near the Bukit Bintang monorail station (the epicentre of consumerism in KL) is now finished, so all looks spick and span.

The hotel I am staying in is a down-market joint, clean, quiet and close to a monorail station. And it’s as cheap as I’ve found anywhere. The receptionist,  who was on the cooler side of civil last time I stayed here, actually beamed at me when I fronted the desk this time, and the bloke who does the rooms almost vaulted the desk to shake hands and greet.

When I went back to the the Estana Curry House to eat, something I have done almost every evening and morning since I started coming to this place, it was as though I had never left. The menus were a little tattier, but the welcome from the waiters that I had befriended last time was warm and spontaneous.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/3C9734D5-835F-4504-9FC1-A15570A73829.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The waiters here work 12 hour shifts, so while they look bright and shiny at breakfast time, say 9am, by dinner time at 8pm they look distinctly tarnished. They may have an hour’s travel on each end of the shift as well...hmm, doesn’t leave much time for anything other than sleeping.

Speaking of eating, here’s the breakfast I like to have. An egg roti (a crisp pancake with egg) orange juice, which is mostly ice, and a coffee, brewed coffee with a hefty slug of sweetened condensed milk.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/B9266D08-8B52-40D1-9123-EE218038CEFC.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The sauces are spicy. The whole deal costs a couple of dollars.

I had lunch one day at a more upmarket place. Actually it was in a basement whose walls were dark wood panelled and whose qualitonious ambience was characterised by discreet downlights. The lunch was a special lunchtime promotion...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/1764D391-F681-470E-B37E-039D034165AA.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The fish is slightly warmed up slices of tuna with spicy angel-hair, (or should that be spicy-angel hair), pasta. It was pretty delish.

On Friday I flew to Kota Kinabalu on Borneo to visit my brother-from-another-mother Andi for a couple of days. Sadly I didn’t get to see his little son (see the last letter from last visit) as he and his mum are in the village a couple of hours away, and she is on the verge of having the next son. So it was just me and Andi and several brothers and sisters who run his massage centre. The massages were great.

He and I ate out both nights...the first in a huge barn-like conglomeration of fish restaurants, where the highlight was a folkloric floor-show involving dancers and fire eaters. The smell of burnt kerosene blended well with the smoke from the barbecues. The second night we went to another fish place attached to the fish market. Once again there were crowds of people, lots of kids and noise and smoke. The food was great...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/image.jpeg' | relative_url }})
![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/EC262058-4B9A-4FB2-89EE-928E1D19138B.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The fish had been sliced down one side of the backbone, then flattened out and barbecued with a mouth-numbing sauce. The meal, with attendant green veg, nasi goring (fried rice), non alcoholic drinks, ended up at about $10 each.

The sunset was pretty spectacular....going down like thunder into VietNam across the, er, bay.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/1FCCAB32-114A-444E-BBC2-80357E9C5309.jpeg' | relative_url }})

And here we are selfying with the obligatory open mouths, showing that life is fun...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/21E7CDCB-7B79-4D1E-87F7-5F78C5D03AC5.jpeg' | relative_url }})

I wandered around one of the giant shopping malls in Kota Kinabalu at one stage, and there was a performance by a troupe of acrobatic types who were doing a show involing dragons...it looks pretty static in the pic, but it swirled and dived and menaced splendidly at the time.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/887EF8AA-073B-4D8D-A229-D964E4900AE1.jpeg' | relative_url }})

And lions...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/2FD83302-D460-4CE2-85ED-8C62464B8B1B.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Each lion had two people inside and they performed amazing routines, often rearing up to 3m tall as the back guy hoisted the front one up, eventually leaping between the little platforms on the tops of the brightly coloured poles in the background. All this to a tinnitus-inducing drum and cymbals accompaniment. Pretty impressive!

There are inevitable language conflicts here from time to time. The myki equivalent (but infinitely less complex but usable in lots more contexts) card is called ‘Touch ‘n’ Go’, which does tell you what to do, but it doesn’t send the most confidence-inducing message to native English speakers.

In one of the shopping centres I spied this shop...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/7C3EDD71-C184-4336-B0B1-FBE558F7D4C7.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Hmm...Cow Dung Tart must mean something else in Chinese!

Whoops...I have just googled the name, and it’s not a quaint double entendre translation. The tarts they make resemble a cow pat...damn clever marketing: here am I doing free publicity for them.

While I’ve been in Koala Lumpa the highlight of the day has mostly been the massage. I have been pummelled and manipulated by this bloke several times. He’s from Myanmar.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/A50ECC7C-28E5-41A0-87CC-13F921F5E560.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The masseurs and masseuses tend to be non Malaysians, some on dodgy work visas, so life for them is pretty hard. They get around $10 an hour for what is hard work, but only for the customers they have...if they have 5 in their shift from around 6pm to early the next morning then they get $50: if they get none they get nothing. They are are identified by numbers, which is impersonal and efficient, but generally have a western name as well. I asked the guy what his name was, and the reply was something like ‘ma’ but that his number was 33. I assumed that he had told me his actual real name, but these blokes speak softly, and I am deaf enough to miss the fine details, and final hard consonants tend not to be pronounced in this part of the world...the name is really Mike. If you are in kl and have had enough of the heat and humidity, and feel like something to revitalise the body, do head to the Sunlight massage centre and ask for no. 33. But do make sure you are not in the restaurant next door, because you’ll only get a nasi goring with a fried egg on top...which does have charms.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/D1EEDBEC-F153-461B-8BEF-BC8FD03D4464.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It may seem that the things I like most about this place are the food and the reception from the people...and that just about sums it up. One is sustenance for the body, the other for the soul.

On Wednesday I fly to Istanbul, going from 32 degree sunniness, to sub 10 degree raininess. Luckily I have packed an umbrella and a warm jacket!

Take care to enjoy life, all of you, and it.

Terry
