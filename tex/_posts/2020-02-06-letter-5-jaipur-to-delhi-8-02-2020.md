Hi all,

After the chaos of the last two cities (Kolkata and Varanasi) Jaipur is an oasis of calm. There are still hordes of motorbikes and tuktuks blaring their admonitions to the world, but the streets are broader so the effect is attenuated.

The place, the old city that is, was laid out by the king, Jai ( hence Jaipur) Singh II in 1727. He and his retinue had lived in the Amber Fort/Palace, on a hilltop in the distance, but decided to build a city on the plain. It is laid out as a rectangular grid, quite a relief after the unplanned nature of the other places. There are parks ( sadly a little desiccated at this end of the winter) and lots of grand buildings. The old city was surrounded by walls and there are 7, I think, gates in. Here’s one....

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2146-7.jpg' | relative_url }})

You may notice the pink hue. The story goes that in 1876 Queen Victoria and the Prince of Wales, confusingly called Albert the same as his dad, arrived on a state visit. The king at the time wanted to impress the colonisers and so ordered that the buildings be painted pink, the colour of hospitality for Hindus. And so it has remained. Puts a new take on painting the town red!

The king also had a whopper palace constructed for them to stay in, now called the Albert Hall. Here’s a pic...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2064-3.jpg' | relative_url }})

There seemed to be no end to the king’s sycophancy. The place is now a museum. This Albert Hall is not to be confused with the one in London, which became a memorial for Victoria’s deceased husband.

In the old city there are miles of small shops selling anything you would want, and shop keepers skilled at selling you what you don’t need.

The airbnb that I’m in is in the new city area and is quite sumptuous. The owners are delightful, he an ex maths teacher and IT person, and she a thoroughly charming woman. They both have excellent English. Breakfast is provided, dinner can be had for a small fee, and the discussions have been great.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/0789A1CF-1030-4C5E-958B-9613B79846F8.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The house was built by her father, a banker, in the 60s and is quite posh. It has been splendid to stay here. The food, as always, is delish.

All these foods are the one price: 20 rupees = 40c.

I went on a 9 hour see-the-sites/sights tour. The Amber Fort was a highlight

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2114-2-2.jpg' | relative_url }})

Inside were splendid columned areas..

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2128-1.jpg' | relative_url }})

It’s not called amber as in colour but a reference to a Hindu goddess I think. The ceiling of this porch, and the audience rooms inside, have masses of curved mirrors on the ceiling, so strategically places lamps, or indeed iPhone torches, cause the whole thing to sparkle like a moonless night.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2131-2.jpg' | relative_url }})

Great gardens...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2129-1.jpg' | relative_url }})

The City Palace was pretty sumptuous. It’s the home of the king, but since democracy came he’s no longer a ruler. The family is massively wealthy and has business interests all over the world.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2091.jpg' | relative_url }})

There’s an observatory here in Jaipur, built in mid 18th century, filled with sundials and other huge devices for determining planets’ positions etc, mainly for astrological purposes...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2076-1.jpg' | relative_url }})
![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2073-1.jpg' | relative_url }})

There are two of these, used for determining which house of the zodiac the sun was in. The other one has the bits of the hemisphere that are missing from this one. The gaps are so the maintenance and cleaning can be done.

Here’s yet another sundial...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2071-2.jpg' | relative_url }})

We also went to the crematorium for the kings. They are burnt here, the ashes taken to Varanasi and the river, then a memorial built on the cremation spot.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2105-4.jpg' | relative_url }})
![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2110.jpg' | relative_url }})
![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2094.jpg' | relative_url }})

It was a most restful and dignified place.

It was a long day, but the guide was good and the experience rich.

On Friday I went to an Indian Cooking class and demo. I was the only one in it. The pair running the show in their kitchen, and I, cooked up a storm of curries and chapattis etc. They were very friendly people, and the whole experience was great...as was the food after.
