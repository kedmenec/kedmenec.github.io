I have been back in Istanbul for a week or so now, and the difference in the weather from Tunis was a bit of a shock to the system. But there have been some sunny days, so when the freezing wind, tornadoing between the buildings threatens to slice you in two, you can feel positive about it. On the gloomy days it is another bowl of soup altogether.

The tram stop near to the place I’m staying is called Çemberlitaş. There is a giant column there, from 1700 years ago, with iron rings around it for stability...and that’s what the name means, a stone (taş) which is with circles (çember+li). I think it may have had a statue on it originally.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/03/3BAF1FD1-9096-424F-9125-EB0E3825ECB5.jpeg' | relative_url }})

I have become friends with several of the shop guys along the street nearby, particularly one shop selling tea, Turkish delight, spices. The owner has taken a bit of a shine to me. He is a big bear of a Turk, with no English, but is always pleased to see me. When I’ve bought things there he charges me the Turkish price, not the tourist price...about 30% less...he says. One day when I walked past they were about to have breakfast, so in the Turkish way, I was asked to join. Here we are...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/03/044F1796-616B-43FF-8422-BD66F2AB2E47.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The owner is on the right. He looks pretty grumpy there, but that’s the older Middle Eastern way. They did tell me the next day that he had had to go to the hospital with some stomach problem, so it is not just that he *looks* like he has a gutsache. Later in the week he was back to smiling.

I often end up walking along the site of the hippodrome, just near the Blue Mosque, where the chariot races were held in Byzantine times 1600 years ago. The area is all pedestrianised now, but some of the stuff from there still remains...this huge obelisk, ripped off from Egypt, covered with hieroglyphics, was a feature when it was a race track.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/03/image.jpeg' | relative_url }})

They did have four huge bronze horses at one end for 900 years or so, but when the 4th crusade, in 1204, on its way to save Jerusalem from the muslims, got side tracked, and ended up ransacking the city, these horses were part of the spoils (along with lots of golden objects pillaged from the biggest Christian church in the universe, Santa Sophia). The horses are now rearing above the facade of St Mark’s in Venice.

Here’s is a quote about the Sack of Constantinople...

‘The sack that followed was one of the worst in all of history. . . No man, woman or child was safe from the ravagers. Robbery and rape were almost universal, mindless destruction widespread. Westerners . . . killed indiscriminately, without mercy or restraint . . . For this to have been done by crusaders - men actually wearing the Cross of Christ - was an ineffaceable disgrace . . . The Greeks never forgot the sack of Constantinople in 1204; its memory, more than anything else, has prevented the healing of the Greek schism from that day to this, despite several major efforts at reunion.’

In 532 there’s was a huge riot in the hippodrome, where two opposing political groups, the the Greens and the Blues, who often sublimated their mutual animosity by supporting their charioteers in the races, got into a stoush, which escalated massively, half the city was wrecked and 30 000 people ended up prematurely dead. It’s called the Nika Riot.

Why am I telling you this? One of the guys in the place I often go to for breakfast showed me a video on his camera of supporters of his football team (say Fenerbahçe) crowded into one section of the stadium, and the rest of the seats empty. The story is that the supporters get into brawls after the matches which are high intensity affairs, and people have been killed. So...everyone but that team’s supporters were let out after the match, and police kept the remainder in their places. Then, maybe 2 hrs later, the stadium was cleared. They filled in their time by massed singing of the team’s songs. It seems that the problems of 1500 years ago remain.

The solution to the problem in the emperor Justinian’s day was to isolate the rioters in the stadium, then send in the army to massacre thousands of them. Hmm...maybe things have developed a bit.

On Saturday I bussed across to Ereğli, a town on the Black Sea, six hours away. A longtime friend, Kadir, lives there with his family and extended family. I am an honorary uncle to his first daughter Betül, who is now 5. I couldn’t visit last year because he and his wife were in prison for political reasons, but they are out now. My reception here is always very warm. Here we are with his father-in-law, Ali, starting a ripper meal...

The rest of the meal was grilled fish, caught by Ali’s professional fisherman brother. Pretty delicious.

Here’s Kadir, his wife Tuba and Betül and the new baby Hazal.

Kadir and I spent the day travelling to a nearby city, visiting a friend of his from when they both were in prison ( the friend had been a policeman who had connections with the wrong side of politics, but now owns a café bar)...here we are...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/03/50CA7544-9CC5-4500-99FF-F174D14756CF.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Then we went up into the mountains, past the snow line along a gradually degenerating road, to see a waterfall. The air was so cool and fresh there...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/03/6ECCC493-24CA-4703-9CD6-8D0FAA62811F.jpeg' | relative_url }})

At the end of the day we had a fantastic meal of fish, including hamsi (fresh anchovies coming from the Black Sea which we were right next to) bigger fish, some slices of a big fish, calamari and shrimps.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/03/BF778152-33A2-4A80-9524-5D6B3342FE4F.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Kadir is a fine man, and he and the whole extended family looked after me very well.

The last couple of evenings were spent with the good friends Eray and Sertaç. I had a picnic with Sertaç on the banks of the Bosphorus, eating olives and cheese and bread and beer and solving the problems of the world as the sun went down...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/03/3EF3F74D-5713-45BE-ABC4-40CB8FFD33F0.jpeg' | relative_url }})

And with Eray to a very down to earth restaurant where you write your order on a slip of paper and send it down on a dumb waiter. A while later up it comes...delicious vegetarian food. Then we went to a favourite bar and solved the world problems over another beer,

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/03/E3592258-CCEE-4214-9B96-F608912C4B52.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The plan for moving on from Istanbul has been tweaked a bit. I was going to go to Zagreb next Monday, to stay a couple of nights with a friend from my time learning Italian in Siena 12 years ago, but she invited me to extend the stay by a few days. She and her husband and son have a holiday house on an island in the Adriatic Sea, and we are all going there for a few days, then returning to Zagreb. It should be spectacular.

So the plan is...Zagreb tomorrow (Friday), then continue to Madrid on Wednesday next week. All the Spanish that I thought I had on the boil (pathetically little in reality, but a start) has been displaced by a sadly withered remnant of Turkish. It seems there is only room for one new lingo in this sadly withering brain space. But once in the land of the long white paella it’ll come flooding back, inşallah.

Take care
