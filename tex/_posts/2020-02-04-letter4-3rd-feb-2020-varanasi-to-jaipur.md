On my last day here (Sunday) I went for a long walk northward, through the teeming streets, past the shops and stalls (one bloke I spoke to was selling shirts with proper collars for $2!), dodging the potholes, the cow pats and the motorbikes, then down to the river and back along the ghats (the terraced banks of the river). On the way I was accosted by one of the street barbers, the blokes who set up with a stool on the side of the street and...er...shave anyone who wants one. I had intended going back to the place I went to a few days ago, but, throwing caution to the winds, I sat on the seat. I carefully watched that he put a new blade in the cut-throat razor, and settled back to enjoy.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2020.jpg' | relative_url }})

As he scraped away, I recalled the diseases that could have been on the list...herpes, HIV, hepatitis and others beginning with h. But it would be ok as long as he didn’t break the skin...yes? But then I noticed him dabbing away at some areas which were bleeding...but only flesh wounds. He finished up with a vigorous massage of most areas of the body.

There is a trap to beware of in such transactions, wherein the seller says ‘Just give me what you think it’s worth’. Fortunately I had had a shave in an actual saloon, so knew the going rate (around a dollar) so I dished out the 50 rupees. But, sir, the massage as well! Stiff, I thought, and, accompanied by his remonstrations, strode off, looking a little like Norman Gunston, but with the wounds healing, and the immune system moving into gear. When I got back to the airbnb I slathered on the Thar’s Poultice Ointment (for the treatment of pimples, piles, eczema and bad legs, according to the tin in less regulated times) so that possibly accounts for at least one of the hs.

Here we are before the money didn’t change hands sufficiently...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2023-2-2.jpg' | relative_url }})

As I walked along the ghats I was hailed by the boatman from a few days ago. I checked the wounds on his arms, and in a grandfatherly way suggested he find a less dangerous way of venting his grief. He agreed that that was sensible.

Further on there were some typical sights...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2025-1.jpg' | relative_url }})

I can’t think of a better way to spend a warm Sunday afternoon, sitting half naked in the half lotus position and meditating by the Ganges.

After the frantic pace of the city streets the river is such an oasis of calm. The opposite bank is the flood plane, and the ghats are ordered and clean. Such a contrast!

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2032-5.jpg' | relative_url }})
![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2035-5.jpg' | relative_url }})![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2030-2.jpg' | relative_url }})

At lunch time I went to a new place, down some steps into a rather bleak, but typical, restaurant. The menu had Thali Special for $4, so I went for that. Thali means a round platter, but also the food, served in little bowls on the platter...here it is

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2027-1-1.jpg' | relative_url }})

The food was spectacular, the rotis so melt-in-the-mouth chewy, and I left the joint, gourmandised and with tingling lips. There is the occasional KFC and Hungry Jack’s here. I wonder why.

I’ve seen monkeys from time to time in my wandering, and Santosh, the manager bloke here, said that they sometimes visit.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_1997.jpg' | relative_url }})

I had been chatting with a tuktuk driver nearby most times I passed, and decided to use him for the 1+hr trip to the airport. It was pretty hair raising.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2020/02/img_2037.jpg' | relative_url }})

Tuktuks are three wheel jobs with an open cabin, steering like a motor bike and a horn that sounds like a strangled cat. But they are very manoeuvrable and the drivers are amazing. I suspect that if I’d taken a taxi the trip would have been longer, the air would have cleaner and the proximity to the end of existence less immediate: but not as much fun. It was a bit ... no, a lot... like dodgem cars at the show, without the crashes!

The plane trip to Jaipur was easy although boarding, which should have been through gate 2, somehow morphed into gate 1, even though the sign on gate 1 said ‘Mumbai’. I found the PA announcements unintelligible (a woman speaking heavily accented English at breakneck speed at a pitch that would be audible to a bat and not much else!), so I probably missed the change. But as the takeoff time approached (running late I rationalised) I did hear my name amid the cacophony and was raced by various officials across the 300 m of runway to be the last on. Phew!
