There’s another language thing that makes me smile here. Mostly people do the th thing in gracias= thankyou, so it comes out as ‘grathias’ where the accent is on the gra, and the s is almost swallowed. But there are some who pronounce the c as an s, and put the accent on the last syllable, including the s, so it sounds as if they had noticed that you had been skidding down the wet lawn in your white pants.

Apparently there are pockets in the country where they don’t th their cs. Interesting. In South and Central America all the Spanish is pronounced with an s.

I wrote the above in happier times, when the bars were open, and anywhere from 10 in the morning the crowds were out drinking, eating and laughing. The sun is still shining and the orange flowers still make walking in the streets an olfactory delight, but there’s hardly a person to be seen, the bars and shops are shut, the supermarkets disgorge people with bags laden with food, as though they were about to be locked up for a fortnight, which is largely true.

The place has been in lockdown since the weekend, and those that are in the street are a metre and a half apart and there’s no kissing or hugging. There’s not a lot of the virus here yet, but schools are closed and people take precautions.

My school announced on the weekend that it was closed for the fortnight, with no promises of anything after that, so the obvious thing to do was to hightail it out of there. I managed to find a flight and train package for Tuesday (that was on Monday when I did it) and all looked nicely sewn up...but just to make sure of the train leg I went to the station, a mere 45 minute walk through the deserted streets, to print the emailed ticket from a machine that does that. I plugged in the code...and the message came up...NOT KNOWN...

A certain four letter word escaped my clenched teeth. The ‘what would have happened if’ scenario flashed through the mind. What if I had turned up on the day and found that the ticket wasn’t going anywhere? What would that have meant for me as the departure time of the plane in Madrid came close, and I was stuck in Seville?

So I returned home through the deserted streets only a little stressed. The guy at the airbnb helped me negotiate the phone help line, and the message came through again, no such booking exists. When I looked at the website of the train line....no such train service existed either!

I hunted around other websites, with trembling fingers and a constricting throat, and eventually found a ticket on the bullet train that does the trip in 2 and a half hours, when a car would take up to 6. Sounded good. The credit card was bleeding already from the plane ticket, and now was very tattered. Made the booking...then waited for the agency to send the ticket. After a while it came...but the wrong one. It was for some character who was travelling in Portugal!

The panic mode was in full fright or flight mode, and I fired off texts, emails, phone calls...until I got an actual human, to whom I expressed my mounting anxiety, and when she fixed everything I apologised. Some glitch, she said, tapped some keys, and within a millisecond there was the ticket.

But...you have to have a hard copy of the ticket, so the page said, but I saw people flashing their phones at the ticket checkers, so I’m not sure about that. Anyway, I wasn’t taking any more chances with it. I trudged through the streets again as the sun was sinking and the homeless types under the shop verandah were tucking their meagre possessions around themselves. Back to the station, put the code into the machine....and....out came the ticket. Whew.

So here I am on the bullet train at 7.30 am travelling at the speed that the Very Farce Train from Kerang to Melbourne can only dream of. In my carriage there are 4 people...travelling is not high on the agenda for Spaniards at the moment. It’s very smooth and quiet and the countryside flashes by in a blur. We overtake the cars on the adjacent freeway (probably travelling at over 120 km/hr) as though they were standing still.

So that's it from texinturkey. All the plans of getting back to Turkey and spending some time with my good friends there, gone...and so on. Still, I’ve had a great time and when there’s no alternative, what can you do?

When I get back I’ll be locked up in the house for a fortnight (even though the word quarantine refers to forty days...er...and nights) and with any good luck and vigorous hand washing before hand, I wont be infected.

I really feel for the lives that have been so massively disrupted by this outbreak. Friends of mine in Istanbul and Kuala Lumpur who have businesses are facing a bleak short term future. When they really only survive on the tourist trade, then there is no way to pay the rent, pay the loan instalments, feed themselves or their families. I can't imagine what the scene will be like in India when the virus spreads; existence is pretty tenuous for a large proportion of the population in good times, and these aren’t those.

Well, after a bumnumbing 24 hours or so in an Emirates jet, Ill be back in Melbourne, stay the rest of the night in a hotel and get the train to Kerang. That’s the plan anyway.

Take care inthese infectious times

T
