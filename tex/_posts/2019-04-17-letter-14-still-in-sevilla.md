Well...Semana Santa (Holy Week) is in full swing and the processions are immense. The crowds are everywhere, the incense is too, and the music and drums fill the aural space. It is really something.

Each of the 70 brotherhoods in the city, associated with churches, has two or three pasos, the floats showing scenes from the passion and one of Mary togged up as the queen of heaven, surrounded by masses of candles. Some of them are 300 years old! They all participate in the Holy Week processions.

In one procession that I watched, there were perhaps 200 of the brotherhood members processing first. They were dressed in traditional gear, with a pointy hat, and are described as penitentes...they are doing the procession as expiation for their sinfulness I expect.

They were followed by a float, sitting on the shoulders of 30 or more costaleros, the guys who do the lifting. Here are some having a beer to recover...this bloke’s neck is in a bit of a mess

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/B8F8F30A-12EE-4DA8-99D9-26736878581B.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Here’s one of the floats...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/7F799323-F8F4-47F1-A217-4B7C04E4785B.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Then more penitents, then a band (around 80 players) some playing a special instrument called a Carmen Cornet, which is like a regular cornet but with one valve to change the pitch by a semitone...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/6D9AFE11-7413-4CD0-B266-9A367FABAAC7.jpeg' | relative_url }})

as well as tubas, trumpets, French horns, lots of drums, saxes, trombones and lots of noise. Then more penitents, another float, then a choir of men and kids singing what sounded like some renaissance music, accompanied by flutes, oboes and bassoons.

Then the Mary float...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/BEBB3404-DE17-445F-89D0-EFD19F75E5FB.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The procession moves for 50 m or so then stops, giving the blokes carrying the floats a bit of a rest I suppose, so the whole show took over an hour to pass. The Mary float, clearly the climax of the event, stopped while a second brass band, which was following, played a minor key dirgey sort of march...the thirufers produced masses of incense, and then just as the band modulated into a triumphant major key section, the float lurched to its feet and continued. The spectators cheered and clapped...it was a pretty amazing vibe. These continue every day and night this week, each church doing its procession along a designated route, reaching the cathedral, then processing back home. Those coming from the outer suburbs may have a 12 hour journey in total. Amazing!

On Wednesday the mother of the Airbnb that I’m in invited me to her mother’s place, on the first floor of an apartment block, with a balcony, to see the procession from on high. Unfortunately there was the hint of rain, so the 4pm parade down that street was called off.

At night it is a bit more atmospheric as it is all lit by candles.

The routes are now well and truly coated with the candle wax, so the tyres on the cars that use the street later utter screams, like a bandaid being pulled off, whenever they turn a corner.

I visited a couple of interesting places, one a private home, but now open to the public. The Palacio de los Nuñas has a history back to the 13th century when it was a convent. Now it is some duke’s place, but downstairs is open. Lovely courtyards...

The other place was at the Plaza de España, a huge building built for the 1928 World Fair, but now government offices.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/8FA17772-D552-4B1A-9665-2125984491D0.jpeg' | relative_url }})

And the attendant gardens were great...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/9CF9B6DA-5C25-4C34-924F-826F627B9A18.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The book that the opera Carmen is based on is set here. The cigarette rolling factory that Carmen worked at (the story is fiction, but the factory isn’t) apparently employed several thousand rollers and produced 70% of the cigarettes sold in Europe. The building is enormous, and is now used by one of the unis...not for rolling smokes though. It could be a profitable enterprise though, smoking is a major pastime here.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/48117CC1-CB71-47D4-BD89-946BFEFB9B2C.jpeg' | relative_url }})

I went to the nearby bull ring, presumably where the toreador that Carmen has the hots for plied his trade.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/4D5EFE3F-CAB8-44B2-A7ED-DBD6B1CD9905.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The other bit of famous opera related to here is The Barber of Seville by Rossini. I haven’t been gauche enough to go into a hairdressers and ask if Figaro is in the shop.

I’ve probably been here in Sevilla a few days longer than necessary, but the Airbnb is quite relaxed and friendly, and I’ve become un buen cliente, or so the waiters told me, at a café/ bar near here. When I arrive it’s always something like ‘hey caballero, que tal?’ (Hey Sir, how are you?) and smiles and handshakes. The food for the set menu lunch is pretty ordinary...the mixed salad being a mountain of shredded iceberg lettuce with some other veg to break the monotony, then fried calamari or whatever. They have paella too, but the idea of a huge plate of rice, no matter how delish, is a bit daunting in the middle of the day. But the reception is good, so I go back now and then.

So, on Good Friday I trundle over the waxen streets to the Railway Station to get the high speed rocket to Madrid. Then on Monday back to Istanbul.

Hasta luego,

T
