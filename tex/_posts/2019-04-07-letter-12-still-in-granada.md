Hola todos, I’m still in Granada, but tomorrow I head to Morocco. The weather has become very cold and wet, with scadded showers and glowering skies. It is a welcome (!) change from the sunniness of the last few weeks...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/FCC6FE86-9DD6-4B8C-8156-5BFDFBBE12B1.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Now, if one is getting a bit overheated, the rain starts and all is cooled off. It’s a perfect system really. In the mornings it has been around 5 or 6 degrees, and one morning my hands were so cold that I was on the lookout for a well-digger, so I could warm my hands on his relatively warm bum (those of you who are unacquainted with the expression ‘colder than a well-digger’s bum’ will probably find that a little curious at best, and deviant at worst) but I had no success there.

The pedestrian crossings here are interesting. The ones without lights are like sacred ground...if you even start to have the thought of using one while standing nearby, the cars will come to a screeching halt. The ones with lights have a timer which counts down the seconds, and the little green man who walks during the first part of the countdown starts to run in the last 5 seconds!

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/13C92223-C918-45C9-949E-38B3587433D5.jpeg' | relative_url }})

On the opposite side of the ravine from the Alhambra, there is a region where Muslim and the Gypsy communities used to live. As you might expect, the streets are narrow and winding...and steep, but the views over the city and the Alhambra are great. It is a bit of a walk to get there.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/image.jpeg' | relative_url }})

There are a lot of cave houses in the gypsy area, and in the more developed areas Flamenco bars.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/5CBB8174-3DB5-49E2-B470-962AE1CDF7E6.jpeg' | relative_url }})

On the way up I encountered a fountain/spring which had this message...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/5501F667-B14E-4442-A71F-83D3BC5EBF26.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It says....

*‘How I would like to be the fountain of my neighbourhood*

*So that when you pass and drink I would feel closer to your lips’*

Or...

**’If I were but a fountain here ! To think**

*That I would touch your lips each time you drink.’*

Somehow I can’t imagine anything like that inscribed over a bubble tap in the main drag of Kerang.

It’s the start of the strawberry season now, and they are dead cheap in the supermarkets...around $2 for a half a kilo! And huge and luscious...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/13F1A349-0703-4C92-ADC4-9A7D4B27680C.jpeg' | relative_url }})

I do wonder how they get to be so big and luscious, but I don’t think it’s related to my growing an extra head!

The practice of going to the same place to eat is paying off well here. At the restaurant I go to, the second glass of wine is on the house, and in the bakery where I’ve had a coffee and croissant most mornings, I get a beaming smile and my order is recited before I’ve had a chance to do it myself.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/9920D0B0-C4D4-40A9-9432-12AD132D9761.jpeg' | relative_url }})

I’m not all that thrilled by the disposable cup, but the coffee is fine.

There is a museum of the Inquisition here, just as there was in Córdoba. It has the same torture machines and descriptions, and I do wonder why I went in. But the thought did strike me seeing the red-hot pincers, the wheel and the barbecue, the instruments of the martyrdom of St Agatha (whose breasts were cut off), St Catherine (who was broken on a wheel) and St Lawrence (who was burnt to death on a gridiron and who is now the patron saint of cooks) because of their beliefs...were the same instruments (among others) that the Church used to martyr those they chose to identify as heretics. Hmm.

It must seem that I a man scratching around for things to do on this cold and wet Sunday afternoon.thats the reality.

Next instalment will be from Morocco if all goes according to plan.

Ciao for now,

T
