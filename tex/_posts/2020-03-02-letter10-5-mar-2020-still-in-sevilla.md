Hi everyone,

Another thing that makes understanding what Spaniards speak difficult is the equivalence of b and v. They both come out as b. So the verb to visit is written visitar, but sounds like bisitar. I asked the teacher how kids learn which is which, because Spanish is pretty much phonetic, unlike English, where the spelling is a nightmare (even though politicians seem to think that phonics is the only way to learn spelling). She said that when she was young, there *was* a difference, but the Academy which makes the rules on what Spanish is, has responded to what was clearly happening in the way people spoke, by declaring that they were the same. So now kids just have to learn which is which. I can remember our family word for the verandah was ‘browndah’ so this b/v thing is pretty natural. She said that kids don’t have spelling lists or dictation, because there’s not much need...quite the opposite of English or French. Another difficulty in understanding is that j is pronounced like an h, h in a spelling isn’t pronounced, g in front of an e or i comes out as an h...most sentences sound like a mess of h and th.

The symbol of Sevilla is

**no8do**.

It’s everywhere that the city has control of...buildings, flyers, bridges...and manhole covers...j

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2020/02/78B9B2FE-C56B-41D7-967D-90D7766E9831.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The ‘aguas y saneamiento’ means ‘water and sanitation’. But what of the no8do?

Here’s a quote from the internet

‘Back in the 13th century, [King Alfonso X The Wise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_X_of_Castile) struggled to fend off an attack by his own son, Sancho. In a bitter battle for the throne, Seville stayed loyal and Alfonso rewarded the hottest city in Spain with a slogan to celebrate its fidelity.’

So his slogan was no8do.

The 8 represents a skein of wool, which in Spanish is ‘madeja’. So ‘no madeja do’. Doesn’t actually mean much, but it does sound like ‘no me ha dejado’ which does mean something... namely ‘she has not abandoned me’. How good is that? As slogans go it is impressive, so our Prime Minister should be impressed too, being an advertising man...but ‘no do’ is a bit close to the mark for him to spin it otherwise!

There’s a great Gothic church near here, in the area named Triana. It’s called Santa Anna, that chick being the mother of Mary according to some non-canonical books that lurk in the larger Christian mythology. It’s pretty nice.

When I have been in India and Turkey I have generally been able to extract a smile and a greeting from people I’ve locked eyes with. There the accepted eye contact time is long enough to get the smiling muscles into go-mode, and the eyebrows to elevate. I remember that when I was in Sienna in Tuscany 15 years ago the ‘look time’ was considerably longer...long enough to get you thumped if you did it in Swanston St. But here there is very little eye contact from passers by...certainly not long enough to get the greetings into gear. I have greeted some old guys, and had smiles and greetings back, but that’s about it. One can end up feeling like an observer rather than a participant...even the waiters standing outside restaurants, supposedly to convince people to come in, don’t maintain contact after the first microsecond. They need some lessons from Turkish spruikers on how it’s done!
