Well.. the train *was* fast...cruising speed of 247km/hr, steaming through rolling hills covered in greening crops, olives, grapes, and stopping in a couple of places. The tunnels were a bit of a test for the compromised Eustachian tubes, but the pain was only momentary.

Córdoba has an ancient centre and a modern outside, but I didn’t venture outside the centre. It was a Roman settlement, taken over by the Visigoths, then a great centre for the Islamic empire which ran from around 700 until 1200 and which included most of today’s Spain. Around 1000 it was one of the most advanced cities in the world in terms of population, learning (botany, medicine, mathematics, astronomy, philosophy), politics. The Muslim rulers supported the Christian and Jewish populations, and all was civilised. Then in around 1200 the empire began to crumble through poor and fractured leadership, and the Christian king Ferdinand III of Castille in 1236 managed to conquer the place.

It’s a great place to wander, many of the streets are narrow and winding, with no or negligible foot paths, and mostly no cars.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/370FC122-6E07-46B0-BC05-C805BCC96E81.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Occasionally the great doors in the walls are open and you can see the splendid courtyards within...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/A70912FC-A927-4A67-A872-0D840339A529.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Sometimes the streets are side-lined with orange trees, which are blossoming now, filling the air with heady perfume...often debouching into parks or squares where the plane trees, still bare in Madrid, are sporting springtime greenery.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/1BD40802-29E4-4612-B081-4BFB69FBD759.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Some streets are lit up with colour...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/73205D76-5F05-468A-B45E-7C06A0805B62.jpeg' | relative_url }})

In the central plaza on Sunday afternoon there was lots of action, kids running through the fountains, clearly obeying their grandmothers who were shouting encouragement (I assume) from the sidelines, and a few kids who were experimenting with making huge bubbles. This little tiger in pink did it for the half hour that I sat and people watched...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/845BFC79-7A05-4EF4-9629-9F3DD4DF9BFD.jpeg' | relative_url }})

There were all ages of people there, strolling, chatting, drinking in the cafes that spill out into the area.

There’s a Roman era bridge over the Guadalquivir river...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/99E26B39-D7D1-4CF0-A653-1339BC6D5766.jpeg' | relative_url }})

On Tuesday I went for a visit to the Mesquita...the name would suggest that it was a little mosque (or a female mosquito) but the reality is the opposite. It would have to be on the list for any traveller as a highlight of monument visiting.

It was a mosque started in 850 and extended over the years until the end of the empire, when Cordoba fell to the Christians. It is huge...180metres x 150 metres.

The outside is made of honey coloured rock, with occasional ornate doors...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/0B359355-7BE7-4DCF-99B0-B540C69A945C.jpeg' | relative_url }})

There is a big courtyard, originally with fountains for ritual ablutions, but now planted with palms and orange trees, and surrounded by cloisters, dominated by a massive bell tower, inside which nestles the original minaret...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/483ABA34-0666-40E3-9B55-EEB68CF5570D.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Inside looks like this...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/862964A8-E9D9-4704-8693-F5BBD75FB607.jpeg' | relative_url }})

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/F2747B71-2F54-40D7-8BD1-F8BD5251D797.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It could hold 40 000 people, and is immense. It has a fabulous atmosphere of calm. It has a good smell also...incense. After the Christians took over the city in 1236 they, over the next century or so, remodelled the interior, plonking a big chapel (actually a cathedral because it is the seat of the bishop) right in the centre of the place. This effectively ruins the unity of the place, and compared with the simplicity of the mosque parts is like an Italian birthday cake...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/9E6D5D45-37C3-4B6E-A958-0D33D57B93AD.jpeg' | relative_url }})

And the wonderful incense smell comes from the smells and bells which are enacted there every day, and have been since it, the cathedral, was interpolated.

Here is a bird’s eye view of the brochure...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/58DDE3BB-E27B-4041-8BF8-E31B15091097.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It is a magnificent place.

The other ancient place here is the ‘Alcazar of the Christian Kings’, which is a big fort just near the Mesquita. Inside it is interesting enough, in a forty sort of way, but the major attraction is the gardens. There are fountains, box hedge enclosed formal sections, stuffed with roses and other plants, ready to splash the joint in colour next month.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/178DF071-6411-43F4-B6F4-9018D5B465D9.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It was great to sit with the warming sun on the back, listening to the splashing of the fountains and watching the passing parade.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/image-1.jpeg' | relative_url }})

There is a torture museum here too. It has examples of the machines and practices that the Inquisition, and other institutions bent on ensuring orthodoxy, used, but the fact that many of the illustrations showing how the devices were utilised have scantily clad pneumatic young women as subjects gives an indication of the tone of the place.

The experience of travelling here is so different from doing it in Asian countries. Here there is next to no interaction with the locals apart from quite civil and sometimes warm commercial transactions in shops and restaurants. I have tried giving a smile to several old chaps sitting in the parks, but each time they look quickly away to the other side of the road, and even if you stand aside to let the other person pass, there is rarely a smile or a reaction. So it is lucky that there are a lot of wonderful things to look at and to eat.

I went to a couple of concerts. One was of cello and piano, playing sonatas of Bach and Beethoven, and the Rachmaninov Vocalise. It was in a small theatre and splendid. The other was the Cordoba Symphony Orchestra playing things of Monteverdi, Mozart (his first symphony, written when he was 8...it was a bit like a kid’s picture stuck on the fridge) Scarlatti and Haydn (symphony 104...nothing like a picture on the fridge!) It was in a much bigger space...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/6F3B2027-5F2F-413A-84F5-75155AB90F5C.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Córdoba is a splendid place to visit.
