The bus from Córdoba to Granada was a little less rapid than the train trip of last letter. The rolling hills of olives and grapes started to be punctuated by seriously high hills, and finally, as a backdrop to Granada, snow capped whoppers.

After the relative quietness and carlessness of Córdoba, Granada on a Friday evening was noisy and crowded. Around the Plaza Isabel la Católica, where two major streets conflue and the motor scooters don’t have mufflers, it was pretty intense. So, relatively jaded after the trip, I wasn’t all that positive toward the place, but during the week all that passed.

On Saturday I went to the Alhambra, the medieval and later fortress 20 minutes walk up the hill from the centre of town. This is the premier attraction of the city and a stunner of a place. The gardens are modern restorations but are superb. There are all sorts of flowers and roses, so in summer the smell must be amazing.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/5770E9A9-DD4E-4574-8E1E-AFA57401A8D2.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Fountains and pools are everywhere, especially in the Generalife Palace. It sounds a bit like a life insurance business, but isn’t! It was a pleasure retreat for the Moorish kings in the Middle Ages.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/04/74F57609-411F-4A3F-8CDA-C8C82EB2E6CC.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The Nazaries palace is the grand finale of the place. When you buy the entrance ticket on the internet you have to book in for a particular time, because they limit the number of customers at one time. So, at 4.30 in I went. It is a stunner of a place, a series of rooms and courtyards, with fountains and decorations and views over the city...

Holy Week is a big time thing here and there are processions at night with groups of guys carrying the pious object graven images through the streets with music and candles and costumes. I will be in Seville when it all happens, but on Sunday afternoon I spotted a rehearsal for the Granada extravaganza....

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/04/0A57635A-8640-453A-830A-4A5BF7E70DA3.jpeg' | relative_url }})

On the day there will be a life size statue with all the trimmings of the Blessed Virgin, or Jesus in agony or whatever that particular church has as its statue du jour, on top. From the way the heavy lifters were shuffling, I’d say the whole affair is a bit of mortification of the flesh. From the size of the blokes it looks like there is plenty of flesh to mortify too!

At the Airbnb I’m staying at one cannot use the kitchen, so I’ve been obliged to get meals in cafes. Breakfast is an interesting time, as the usual one for the locals is a churro and hot chocolate, or a croissant and a coffee. I tried the churro one morning...it’s a bit like a long thin doughnut, a stream of batter that’s drizzled into a cauldron of hot oil. So inside is mostly air, and the outside is a chewy, oil infused bit of crunch. The hot chocolate sticks nicely when you dunk.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/04/ABB9D340-8CE3-4EB9-BEB4-6E71157EC22E.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Each of those is over 30cm long, so that’s on the obese side of a metre and a half. I was pretty much over it by the time I reached the midpoint, but had to continue to the bit of end. The churrerías, the places where they do it, are everywhere, and the one I have been to a few times is the Alhambra, on a square near the cathedral, where the waiters are very simpatico and the atmosphere is great.

Bull fighting in Spain is still a national spectacle, and here in Granada there is a big arena. It sits on the appropriately named Plaza de Toros...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/04/5FEF736D-1702-4991-BB37-17D1A23421F7.jpeg' | relative_url }})

But the whole affair is, as you could imagine, rather politically charged. One graffitist made his views know one side...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/04/351FBFEB-0FD3-4EE8-B329-D1D1F8B53B1F.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Tonto (yep the Lone Ranger’s offsider) means idiot, dope, stupid.

I went to one 20 years ago in Valencia, and left it feeling very compromised. Like the churros, once is more than enough!

One the way up to the Alhambra there is a statue commemorating a late 19th century Spanish writer named Angel Ganivet. It has the nudist grasping a goat’s horns, and seemingly wrestling with it. One commentator that I read suggested that, in the absence of any stated explanation of the iconography, it could be the artist fighting with the untamed forces of change. Hmmm. Anyway, the stream of water seems to be unrelated to the story, but from certain angles does look a bit curious.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/04/706CFD9F-E14D-4B28-B642-8E48067E22E1.jpeg' | relative_url }})

We, the inheritors of the Northern European aversion to saying the name Jesus, except in ecclesiastical contexts, have a bit of a reality check here where Jesús (pronounced Hey soos) is a pretty common name (likewise in South America and the Philippines). Here’s an example...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/04/15FE7AB0-0FE4-4129-8B98-0EB4485FD04E.jpeg' | relative_url }})

I took a trip to Málaga on Tuesday. It’s about an hour and a half southish from Granada, and is a seaside port and windsurfing place. My main reason for going was my memory of the name in Ernst Toch’s piece for spoken ensemble called ‘geographical fugue’ where the line ‘Canada, Málaga, Rimini, Brindisi’ gets repeated often. Here is a link if you want to hear it...

<https://youtu.be/jMOLiFcPvgI>

Anyway, the town didn’t appeal all that much but the cathedral was a stunner...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/04/DC1A3A1C-1557-480E-BA58-3599C6547DF7.jpeg' | relative_url }})

I have frequented a restaurant in the old part of town, near the cathedral, which has only room for a dozen or so people, and which does the usual set menu for lunch, and more elaborate items for dinner. The two waitresses are splendid...one, a 20 something Spaniard is lively and appealing, the other a slightly older Frenchwoman who slips from French to deliciously accented English to Spanish (I don’t know how delicious that is, but I can only fantasise) with absolute ease. On Wednesday I arrived for dinner at around 8pm and had to wait as they were in the process of opening, so I went to a nearby plaza, and had a beer and watched the passers by for a while.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/04/753C9913-5C52-494F-B80C-3CF6618132EC.jpeg' | relative_url }})

In Granada, and I assume elsewhere, when you get a drink in a bar it comes with a tapa (a snack) as well...it could be olives or cheese or nuts or ....in my case some tortilla...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/04/99F02BE2-7767-4D56-A509-61817AA6FEC2.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Then I returned to the restaurant to have salmon followed by chocolate cake. The evening was warm, the reception in the restaurant was likewise, and the food was splendid.

Debussy wrote a piano piece called ‘Evening in Granada’ in which scraps of the evening’s sounds are wafted on the errant breeze. It is most evocative. In my case the breeze brought the sounds of an old accordionist playing some tangos, but not changing the harmonies as often as musical taste would decree, the distant sound of motorscooters, and the totally seductive half-heard conversation of the French couple on the adjacent table. Ay caramba!

My next trip is from here to Tarifa, right down near the rock of Gibraltar. From there I’m going to Morocco for a few days. My big problem emerged when I finally got around to planning the Here to Tarifa travel. The public transports were all highly indirect and took far too long, so I started to investigate the ride-sharing app called BlaBlaCar. It’s a bit like sophisticated hitchhiking. If you are driving somewhere and want some paying company you put your details on the app. If you want to go somewhere then you consult the app and choose the ride you want. Easy peasy.

So I went to Malaga with Alvaro the communications freelancer in his aging little car, and returned with Miguel the executive type in his BMW. That 90 min trip (shorter on the return journey because the Beamer could hit 140 km/hr on the freeway with no visible sign of stress) cost $14 or so. On Monday I’m going with Macarena (the driver not the dance) to Tarifa, the 3 and a half hour trip costing around $33. It’s a great system.

The splendid weather seems to be coming to an end...rain is forecast for the next week. Ah well, I’ve got an umbrella.
