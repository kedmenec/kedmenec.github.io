Hi all,

I had another encounter which shows up reasons why spoken spanish is a bit hard to deal with. I was talking with an old guy at a stall selling sunglasses and belts, and I ended up buying a belt from him for 5 euros (around $8). He seemed to want a chat and he asked where I was from. I told him, then asked him the same question...it’s always good for a laugh, and he identified as a Sevillano.

He told me his name, which was Domingo. Strange I thought, that means Sunday. Then I realised that the guy whose place I’m staying in is Italian, and his name is Domenico, which is just the boy form of Sunday for him. And there’s Placido Domingo (lazy sunday I guess)

So I told him my name was Terry, being very careful to enunciate the T...not a good move. ‘Cherry?’ he asked. I repeated, but still he said ‘Cherry’ and so I left it at that. It was only later that I realised that a hard English T sounds just like a Ch to Spaniards, as they pronounce T as a soft thing sounding more like a th, with the tongue almost between the teeth. So next time it will be Therry, and we’ll see. Maybe it is this absence of clear consonants that makes Spanish sound pretty lazy at times.

The classes have been going well, and I’m progressing well.

The other big thing is the CV, which is getting to the pointy end now. There have been a couple of cases in Sevilla, but lots more in the North. Schools are closed for the next two weeks, there’s no hand sanitiser to be found anywhere. I did see a woman buying three 5L bottles of oil, but maybe she was running a massage parlour.

Travel is limited a bit; there are some areas in the country where entry and exit is prohibited.

What will happen in the next 3 and a bit weeks when I want to go to Madrid, then back to Istanbul is totally unknown. I do feel like a frog in a heating beaker of water, but I’m not making any decisions. Issues like cancelled flights, rearranged timetables, quarantine, getting sick...are too difficult to worry about. But there’s plenty of toilet paper, so all is well for the time being.

Hm. A short letter today.

Stay well everyone

T
