Hi everyone,

Morocco, if all goes to plan! Well I’m in Sevilla, four days earlier than planned. And Morocco has been scratched from the list.

So what went wrong?

The public transport route from Granada to Tarifa (the port where the ferry for Morocco leaves) turned out to be very tortuous...a 12 hour trip, changing trains/buses blah blah which should have taken only 3 hours. So I took the BlaBlaCar ride-sharing option, and found the one and only ride going there, leaving at 5.45 am (!) from the Plaza de Toros one day after I really wanted, but I coped with that. So, bright eyed, relatively, and bushy tailed, I walked the 30 minutes from the Airbnb to the said Plaza, glad that I had the umbrella with me as the drizzle had started, through the empty streets of Granada at a time when anyone with any sense was still in bed. Reached the meeting point, expecting that Macarena, in the Peugeot, would arrive on the dot and I’d be on my way.

Well...after an hour of waiting, two sms and unanswered phone calls later, I finally bit the bullet. The plans in tatters, and the impossibility of making the connection with the already booked ferry, I walked the 30 more minutes to the central bus station and bought a ticket for Sevilla. The credit card did send little twinges as the forgone accomodation in Morocco (which was unrefundable at this late stage) was forwent, and the extra four nights in Sevilla were acquired, but what the heck! I sensibly muttered...it’s payday soon! So a short cut in the plan, and a few extra days here

A note first about pronunciation and spelling of this city’s name...

We have Seville oranges (pronounced as SEvil where I come from),

the place in Victoria, I imagine, poshes it up a bit by moving the accent to the second syllable, so seVIL.

And so we come to this place. Here it is spelt Sevilla, and the ‘v’ comes out as a ‘b’, and the ‘ll’ Is pronounced like a ‘y’ (Mallorca sounds like Mayorca, paella sounds like paeya) so we get ‘ SeBIya’.

English never seems to care much what the locals call their cities, or indeed countries...Roma turns into Rome, Firenze turns into Florence, Napoli turns into Naples. It’s not alone though...Turkish has some great ones: what Greeks call Hellas, we call Greece and Turks say Yunanistan, we say Croatia, Croatians say Hrvatska and Turks say Hırvatistan.

The b vs v thing is very widespread too. The letter in Greek that looks like a B (ie a beta) is pronounced as a v, so Melbourne comes out as Melvourney. In Spain there is a wonderful one...mobile is written as móvil and pronounced mobil! Maybe it makes sense...movable and mobile probably come from the same Greek root.

I went to the Alcazár or fortress/palace here. It was originally a Moorish (ie built by the Muslim empire of the Middle Ages here) palace, much like the one in Granada, the Alhambra, and in Córdoba, but after the Catholic kings reconquered the joint in mid 1200s, was taken over, extended in Renaissance style, then Baroque as the centuries went on. It is now a residence of the king when he comes to visit, but the spare rooms and gardens are open for visitors...and there are millions every year. The queues were long, but the weather was fine, so all was well.

The place is splendid. The Moorish parts are in much better state than in the Alhambra in Granada. There are splendid courtyards and tiled rooms...

I didn’t find the renaissance and later parts very interesting. The gardens were extensive, and more park-like than the formal ones in the Alhambra. There were even some peacocks strolling and yowling...

The preparations for the Holy Week processions are very obvious now. Much of the inner city, along the route of the procession, is blocked to traffic and tiered seating is being installed. Churches which are often closed except when mass is on, are open, and the ‘floats’ are on display...(I suspect that the blokes carrying them won’t be calling them floats!)

Palm Sunday is this weekend, then the nightly processions start after that...incense, candles, music, crowds, apparently still going at midnight. It’s a big thing here! Pictures next letter.

I also went to the Cathedral. It’s the biggest Gothic place in the known universe, and the third biggest church. There was originally a mosque on the site, but the catholic kings eventually largely dispensed with it, leaving the ablution courtyard and the minaret, now topped with a belfry, and standing over 100m tall...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/D934F6F2-1DE6-436D-B4F4-CE229245BC0E.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The inside is gobsmackingly huge...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/69BDD8DD-83F5-4438-8407-60F89FCD62F3.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The organ is a monster too. I went to a couple of recitals and was suitably blasted away.

The tomb of Christopher Columbus (called Cristóbal Colón here...sounding somewhat alimentary) is here too...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/C3DAF27E-C516-406C-A167-99C4A2A8B180.jpeg' | relative_url }})

There is a splendid museum of fine arts. It occupies a former convent, and is a most peaceful and excellent place. There are lots of internal courtyards...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/F5144E2D-F313-4921-905B-B3378A0A8389.jpeg' | relative_url }})

...hardly any people, and some pretty bizarre religious art...

Here s a statue of San Ramón Nonatus. He did his thing in the Middle Ages, and worked at rescuing Christians who had been captured and held for ransom in North Africa. The story is that he himself was captured, and to stop him preaching, his captors bored holes through his lips and put a padlock on...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/9978A78E-E419-405D-AB2A-EA63A4810C9C.jpeg' | relative_url }})

His name, no-natus, refers to his being born by Caesarean section (ie not-born) and he is the patron saint of childbirth, pregnant women...and priests keeping the confidentiality of the confessional!

Here is a grizzly detail of a huge last judgement painting...

From the look of what’s also on the menu it is fortunate that they stopped at the bread and wine for holy communion.

The museum is small enough to be enjoyable, and a great place to visit. It is free for EU people, but €1.50 for foreigners. So I fronted up to the guy at the desk with my money in hand and he said ‘it’s free’. Ever honest I said, ‘pero no soy de EU’ and he said words which I interpreted as meaning ‘but you are oldish’!!! In Sevilla many of the attractions are discounted or free for over 65s. There are some advantages.

The weather has returned to excellent, Sevilla is a great place to be.
