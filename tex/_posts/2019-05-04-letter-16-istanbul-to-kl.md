Hi all,

This is the last letter (sigh).

I had a good time in Istanbul...didn’t go to one sacred site and only one tourist attraction.

Under the old part of the city there are lots of water cisterns...huge rooms that were used to store water in the Byzantine times. There are a couple that have been renovated, and are visitable, some that are under hotels and are likewise, but many others that are known about, but not accessed. A new one has recently opened, which doesn’t seem to be present in the touristsphere, and which I was directed to by a tout who later took me to his uncle’s kilim shop, and later his cousin’s ceramic shop.

At the famous one, the Basilica Cistern, the queues are long, and it costs, but this one was virtually empty, was free and quite beautiful...there was atmospheric mood music playing...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/14550A25-3072-4273-8E7A-1136FEEFCA7F.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The rest of the time has been spent catching and recatching up with friends.

One night I went to Kumkapı, which is a fish restaurant place not all that far from my Airbnb, with a travel agent friend Osman, and two of his staff. The name Kumkapı refers to its place near the gate in the ancient city which led onto a sandy beach...Kum means sand and Kapı means gate. There are several places named like this; Topkapı, Yenikapı. The street is lined with fish restaurants which spill out onto the pedestrianised street, there are gypsy bands, lots of noise and smoking and it is very lively.

Here we are...

We later went inside where there was a band and then a belly dancer. It was all very loud, I could almost see the no smoking sign through the smoke, but it was enjoyable.

I spent another day at Sertaç’s place just lounging around, chatting and helping him set up an area on the terrace above. Here’s Anna and him and me lounging in the beautified part...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/286F8347-FDB7-4645-979C-C3ED42D3D069.jpeg' | relative_url }})

They are such easygoing and stimulating people to be around.

I spent another day with my good friend Eray and some of his friends, breakfasting, and then lounging by the sea, having dinner and then going home. Here’s Eray and Mehmet (a very frequent name here) and his wife on the ferry...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/04/B0DDAACB-4BBE-4096-8599-B467B155DCA9.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It was such a pleasant way to live in the moment. And it was great to be included in the group, hearing their stories.

There’s an oldish chap, who has a souvenir and handbag shop near the grand bazaar, that I always spend some time with when I’m here. The first time we came into contact was 5 years ago when I was looking at his window and he said ‘Is there anything you are looking for?’ I replied ‘Just the meaning of life’. He didn’t miss a beat ‘Can’t help you there, you have to find that yourself, but I can show you some handbags’. Since then we chat and share impressions of the absurdity of existence. His name is Jelal. I’m only here once a year, but we continue chatting each time although it’s every week. One time, when we were talking about amassing wealth, he shared the Turkish (and I imagine everywhere else) saying that ‘the last shirt you wear has no pockets’. I felt better about my profligate lifestyle after that.

The political situation here has become interesting. In the recent municipal elections, the party associated with the president, the AKP, was narrowly defeated in Istanbul, and clearly defeated in Ankara, Izmir and other big cities. Last year no one would mention the president critically, and in fact he is a dictator here. The parliament is a rubber stamp for the AKP and the big man. But this year people I was talking to in shops, and they had no idea if I was a spy or not, openly mocked his lack of success. Further adding to the derision is the all too obvious fact that the AKP were convinced that they would win in Istanbul, and had ‘thanks istanbul’ banners and huge ads printed and put up before the final results were announced. So there is the local candidate and the beaming president on huge billboards, thanking the electorate... for defeating them. The cities now in the opposition hands together make up around 60% of the production of the country. There is a swing in the air. The next election will be interesting. When the price of vegetables goes up by a factor of 5 the mood for change is strong.

The trip back to Kuala Lumpur was uneventful except for a computer malfunction at the new Istanbul airport, which meant that the passport control was done by hand...they wrote the name and number on a form, stamped the passport and that was that. I see later that the same kneesup happened in the Australian airports, on the same day. Is it a worldwide trend? I shudder to think so the state of the world if the IT systems everywhere went off!

It was heartwarming, and given that the temperature here ranges between 25 and 36 every day, body warming, to get back to Kuala Lumpur. The guy who cleans the rooms beamed and shook hands, the waiters at the Estana Curry House did the same, the sellers of fake watches near the hotel welcomed me back. It’s like being in a village, this section of Jalan Sultan Ismail. The massages have been excellent.

Well...that’s it for another set of blog. On Sunday I get the budget flight back to Oz, then on Monday the train to Ktown.

See some of you in the flesh in the coming weeks.

Ciao.

T
