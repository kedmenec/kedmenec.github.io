The place I’m staying at is an Airbnb in the centre of town. It’s in a pretty dilapidated 3 storey place, and inside is fairly primitive but ok. The major attractions are the location, and Ahmed, the grandfather of Youssef the organiser. The place is his pied à terre, his flat in the city. His actual home is in a suburb 6 km away. He was an executive in an IT concern, but now, at 82 is retired. His name is Ahmed Meksi...he told me Meksi means ‘dressed’ in Arabic.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/52F10E7C-08AA-4DC3-907D-94664173C579.jpeg' | relative_url }})

He looks a bit of an old man there, but he is trim, energetic and excited about life. We have had some long conversations about stuff...religion, politics, the nature of humanity....and he has been great. He has also given me advice about touristy things which has been invaluable.

One day, for example, I asked him about the Tunis Museum at Bardo, which google maps said was an hour’s walk away. Yeeeees he said, adding that it was around 6km. So I decided to walk. It was ok strolling through the burbs, asking directions from time to time, and getting the bemused response ‘why not get a taxi?’. Eventually, after an hour I checked google maps and found that I was only half way there. Bugger!

When I got there, I realised the inadvisability of wearing ones feet out *before* the museum visit, but as I was wearing sensible shoes it was ok. There was a terrorist attack here in 2015, 21 people were killed, and the bullet holes and shattered display cabinets remain...along with pretty hefty security.

There are lots of Roman mosaics, and a few headless statues and a couple of spectacular rooms...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/E4854560-5415-4412-8DE5-C8907540B60B.jpeg' | relative_url }})
![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/D8E1A40D-2ACE-4D44-B085-697D592E9815.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Rather than walk back, I got a taxi. It cost around $3 and the driver told me I could have got the tram there for about 30c!

There are the usual scams that you find in these places, and I have encountered two. The first is when the youngish bloke joins you as you walk along, talking lots, giving a guided tour, but at the end putting the hard word on you for some money. I am hardened now and tell them to head off.

The second is a little more subtle. In the footpath cafes the waiter might do the trick of telling you the bill, inflated of course, rather than giving you the ticket from the cash register. I was caught twice (only a dollar each time) so now I follow Ahmed’s advice and pay at the till. It is presumably in the café’s interest to do it the right way, because the waiter presumably pockets the whole lot. In one place this sign is visible...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/DADA3220-CDEC-4893-80F8-5544C970419B.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The text says something like...’we ask our dear customers to ask for the cash register ticket before paying’. The scam is well known it seems.

I visited Carthage one day. It’s the archaeological site of the great civilisation that was a rival to Rome in BCE times. Rome defeated them in the third of the Punic wars, and levelled the site, so there’s not a lot to see. In the second Punic war (Punic refers to the Roman name for Carthage), Hannibal, the great Carthaginian leader, took the battle to Rome, shipped elephants across the straits of Gibraltar, then took them and his armies through the south of France, over the Alps and ended up at the gates of the city. He didn’t attack, mostly because a lot of his elephants had perished in the alpine traverse, and without them he couldn’t do the coup de grâce, and was eventually defeated on Italian soil. All these wars happened around 250 to 150 BCE.

The site was very pleasant, on a hill overlooking the sea, the actual museum was closed for renovation, but the huge church, built there by the French 130 years ago, was interesting...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/7157BAD0-32D7-4008-9C65-CA5721CACF67.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It looks a bit like a mosque, or a harem.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/E2132205-E5F5-4A32-9F41-0438FFF35B99.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The place is no longer a church, and they do concerts there in the summer.

Carthage is 25 mins away from the centre of town on the suburban railway. It cost around 30c for the trip. The train ride felt distinctly third worldian, with niceties such as closing the automatic doors before the trip resumed, largely being ignored. But for 30c one can’t expect the 9.54 from Brighton.

A few stations further north is the upmarket town of Sidi Bou Saïd. It’s on a hill and the view over the sea is stunning...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/4514ABD5-D717-49F8-8B57-52389AC5C0F0.jpeg' | relative_url }})

And the sugar cube houses, with blue shutters and trimmings suggest wealth.

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/4A695747-BE79-4237-BB42-D54E9923B907.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The reason for visiting was to see the mansion built at the start of the 20th century by a vaguely eccentric baron from France. It looks over the sea from on top of the hill...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/2E116F42-8944-4310-98BA-FAD60AF2D7EF.jpeg' | relative_url }})

And inside is a wonderful riot of decorated rooms...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/A6F72772-9321-42DA-8820-36916BE2C0AE.jpeg' | relative_url }})
![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/073379F3-F2A1-426A-9C55-D81CF636B672.jpeg' | relative_url }})

There are little bedrooms with balconies, a Turkish bath, large rooms where now classical music concerts are held. A really stunning place. He was an artist and musicologist, and there is a museum of musical instruments, both Western and Arabic. He wrote a huge treatise on Arabic music, which was the standard text until the 60s.

There were gum trees in the gardens and streets...

![]({{ '/assets/uploads/2019/02/9ADEAB35-E1CC-466A-919B-DB4C5FF0ABCB.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It was one of the highlights of this part of the trip.

Well folks, that’s it for a few more days.
