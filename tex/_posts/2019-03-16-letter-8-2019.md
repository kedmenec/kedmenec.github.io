Hi everyone,

It may have seemed from the farewell from last letter that there were no rocks on the trajectory, and that all the plans would not gang awry.

There *were* rocks on the way and I steered right onto them.

Getting to Paris from Zagreb was effortless. Zagreb airport is small and new and a delight to move through. Once I had reached Paris, I had an hour to get to the connecting flight from Paris to Madrid...quite easy to do...but I didn’t realise that there were two flights to Madrid leaving at the same time, but from different terminals. Of course I went with the first one, which was not the one I had booked, and the awful truth was exposed as I tried to board. Bloody hell!

The real departure was from a terminal on the other side of the universe, and after telling my story to half the officials, and eating the packed lunch, I eventually got to the Air France desk and told it once more. But...the type of ticket I had bought had a fee of about $500 to change, and the next flight had to be the next day. Bloody hell again. Then the delightful woman consulted her colleague, and they suggested to me that my flight from Zagreb had been late, and consequently I had missed the connection, wink, wink. Of course, I concurred...nothing to do with my own stupidity. So then they could put me onto the next flight, and there would be no charge. I had gone from seeing any hope of extracurricular activities flying out on the back of a burning credit card, to a fawning mess of merci beaucoup madame, vous êtes magnifique.

All of the people...frenchies...that I dealt with at the airport were terrific. I spoke in English, apart from the initial greetings, and so did they, and they could not have done more to help.

I don’t think anyone saw me drooling when the announcements in the aeroplane came over in French. It is such a luscious language. Why would you want to learn Indonesian for a second language? ( yes I know they are our biggest neighbor blah blah..but is that a decent reason?)

The dramas weren’t over though. Because I was arriving at the Madrid Airbnb much later than planned there were problems getting into the place, and the woman who owned it made several plans, none of which came off. While I was waiting I sat in a dingy bar near to the apartment with the young barman named Alberto, drank several beers, and communicated in broken English and Spanish, as the clock ticked past 9, 9.30, 10...

The bar’s selling point was that they had hookahs, (the bubbly water pipe, not the bubbly other one) and he showed me some of the tricks he could do.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/7F9E52B0-8B30-418E-B585-324D78205EEB.jpeg' | relative_url }})

That’s soap bubbles filled, or not, with smoke. When I seemed totally desperate and getting nowhere with the accomodation he offered to speak to the Airbnb woman, who was driving back from somewhere in the wilds of Spain, and eventually got me into the place. I felt a bit like Goldilocks once I was in bed...what if I was in the wrong place?/room?/street? But all was well. The kindness of strangers in these situations is amazing.

I then had dinner in a vegetarian place around the corner. People eat late here! Here’s the avocado salad...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/857D6A20-CB47-46E2-A218-87199437B6B9.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The salad and two glasses of wine came to about $23 so the bank can handle it.

The restaurant was a cosy joint, with very few customers...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/A6295FEF-CE7A-47D8-A989-FD2BC91FC5C2.jpeg' | relative_url }})

I visited the Cathedral, which is another Gothic Revival style edifice. Looks like a 15 th century place inside, apart from the modern muriels around the altar...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/0A17B0E4-2860-4B43-A65B-95962995836C.jpeg' | relative_url }})

...but was built in the late 19th century, same as St. Paul’s in Melbourne. Somehow St. Paul’s’s (take that, punctuation pedants) neo Byzantine art nouveau muriels seem to fit better than the ones here.

They have a museum containing ecclesiastical gear from the past, particularly heavily embroidered copes and chasubles and other episcopal fancy dress. They were very lush, and heavy too I expect, but all I could think of as I looked was George Pell. The notice said ‘no fotos’ so I wasn’t game to risk one.

Up on the roof fotos were allowed. The view of the city was impressive...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/D877E28D-1C65-4818-8B7C-1FB02A3D6252.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The wind was penetrating, and at least one of the graven images of saints and martyrs, the latter holding instruments of their demise, was confronting...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/1C354C9E-888A-4695-8355-0574E4EABD68.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It’s not all that easy to see, but this joker is holding his own skin, and a knife. He is St Bartholomew (also identified with St Nathaniel, which just goes to show how shrouded in mythology these blokes are), one of Jesus’ gang, who is credited with being flayed alive in the first century CE. He’s the patron saint of tanners. Pretty gruesome.

Madrid is a great city, so tidy, clean, beautiful. The splendid 18th and 19th century buildings, probably financed by the bonanzas coming from colonies in Central and South America, are well maintained, and in the brilliant sunshine look fabulous.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/FE5CDF7B-ED3D-4869-BB09-2A0447521045.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The weather has been excellent, and demonstrations, like Friday’s school kids’ one (it seems they didn’t take any notice of SloMo’s exhortations to stay at school and learn about coal either) and Saturday’s to do with Catalan independence, were good to watch.

I went to the Royal Palace on Saturday, and was suitably repelled by the chandeliers and gold and triumphalism of the place. Here’s a statue of one of the kings, dressed as a Roman Emperor, at the head of the grand staircase...and the ceiling above.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/CE314859-D80E-42BA-8765-362646C18E62.jpeg' | relative_url }})
![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/FBC3512E-71F1-44A6-874F-2DE9ECC7F05A.jpeg' | relative_url }})

There were countless rooms, each more garish than the last.

I did the big tour of the Prado Museum too. A spectacular place, very well organised, and filled with art works that occupy lots of the pages of any art book that you’d look at. Hieronymus Bosch’s ‘Garden of Earthly Delights’ was a great treat.

That’s it for a while. In a week’s time I’ll be in Cordoba south from here, but there’s still lots to explore in Madrid.

All the best

T
