I had thought that the entry into India was going to be a bureaucratic nightmare, having been warned by the internet (who should know), but it was relatively eventless. Still, at 12.30 am there weren’t many other flights, and even fewer candidates in the foreigner queue. Then came the taxi ride from the airport, the incessant horn blowing by drivers who figured that the white line was a guide for them, and that others should get off it, and the sulfur street lights beaming through the smog. Eventually we got to #16 Deodar St Ballygunge...at 2.30am, I was greeted at the gate by the mother of the house, taken down a narrow alley, through a wooden door, up some stairs, met the father of the house, Mr Dasgupta, and into the room.

The hosts are splendid people. The wife is a psychologist and famed classical Indian dancer, and the husband a retired professor of Literature. She has some YouTube clips...Surangama Dasgupta. The daughter is in China at the Indian Embassy organising cultural events (she is also a dancer) and the son is studying anthropology at a uni in Delhi. I have eaten with them most nights...ultra tasty Bengali food... and had splendid discussions about religion and politics!

Kolkata is a bit confronting. The air is at various levels of extreme pollution, the sound of car horns is endless and the traffic moves at a fair clip. There are only two types of pedestrians...the quick and the dead. In the area where I am most of the commerce is done from footpath hugging small stalls...vegetables, street food, fish...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/01/img_1868-2.jpg' | relative_url }})

The fish come from the local rivers. Gasp.

There are big covered markets too which do the food and clothes selling. I chanced upon the meat section of one such place, and it seemed like a vision of hell...sheep’s carcasses hung up and being subdivided, slabs of gleaming flesh and offal....urk.

The street food is rotis with vegetable or chickpeas curries, or rice and various dishes, and also chai...sweet milky tea that you get in small clay cups (although small paper ones are possible too) for 20c. You then hurl the clay cup onto the heap and it returns to Mother Earth. Here’s a cup...

The streets are generally in a state of decay, and travel along the footpath is rather perilous. The telephone wires are generally a massive tangle

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/01/img_1844-2.jpg' | relative_url }})

and the smell of urine a constant in some areas.

It’s winter here, and very dry, so there is dust everywhere and the trees in the street are coated. The max temp has been around 25 degrees, so quite pleasant. In summer it can be 45+ with high humidity.

I went to the massive Victoria memorial...a tribute to Queen Victoria, empress of the country. It is huge and the gardens around are expansive.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/01/img_1869.jpg' | relative_url }})

Inside there’s a lot to do with the queen, and her statue is of a young version, rather than the portly, we-are-not-amused, housefrau that we are used to.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/01/img_1852-1.jpg' | relative_url }})

There are excerpts from her speeches on the walls in English, Arabic, Hindi and Bengali, and the nauseating sentiments (especially given the way the British and the East India Company raped and pillaged and destroyed the economy of the place during the colonial period) sound like the platitudes spouted by our PM on Australia Day.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/01/img_1853-1.jpg' | relative_url }})

I talked with the Dasguptas about the Indian attitude to the British during the colonial period, and the feelings are still very raw.

The GPS is vital for the tourist here as there are few street signs, and off the main drags the streets are labyrinthine. And some of the routes it chooses take you to interesting places. One went through an area reminiscent of Slumdog Millionaire: normally I wouldn’t have ventured into such a place, but the computer said it was ok!

I haven’t been game to travel on the trains or buses here. They are cheap, decrepit, overcrowded and pretty scary looking. So for the long distances in the city I’ve used the Uber which is much better. Even the hour long trek through the suburbs to a rather uninspiring temple on the river only cost $5. The traffic is horrendous, and the drivers I’ve gone with have been amazing in negotiating it. Most of the cars here have dings in the sides, and there is barely a taxi that isn’t scarred in some way.

Tomorrow (Tuesday) I fly to the holy city of Varanasi, on the Ganges. The airbnb I’m staying is called Bougain Villa. I should feel at home there.
