The flight from Istanbul to Madrid was very easy, and I got through passport control and baggage collection in triple quick time. Madrid seems so clean and tranquil after the other places I’ve been: traffic is sedate, very little horn blowing, the sky is clear and vividly blue and the air is clean, cool and invigorating. Here is the Plaza Mayor, a big square that’s surrounded by restaurants and filled with people most of the time.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/BE1117F6-6D6C-43A3-B5A8-7AFAC37F8C15.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The airbnb is a bit eccentric. The room is fine and the bathroom good. The couple who run it are interesting...the husband Aitan is very hospitable and he is willing to be patient as I stumble my way through the Spanish thoughts that I seem to want to express. Claudia, the wife, is a bit full on...her responses to critical comments on the airbnb website are just a tad excessive. I’m actually half afraid to use the bathroom in case my cleaning up afterwards isn’t up to scratch. And although the listing says the kitchen is available I haven’t been game. There is no table in the kitchen, but the floor is massively clean, so I guess that’s where they eat! We have only spoken once, and she was charming then.

There is also a largely hairless chihuahua named Lisa, rather old, and with severe cataracts. The poor dog can’t see much at all, but is clearly loved by the father of the house, Etain.

On Friday night I revisited a bar that I had been to last year. This bar is very dark and atmospheric, and its claim to fame is that you can smoke using nargiles, or bubble pipes, there. At that time I had gone there to fill in time until the woman who ran the airbnb, which was just up the street, returned home. The barman, Arturo, had eventually spoken to her on the telephone, found the key under the mat at the door to her apartment and let me in. What a winner of a young bloke! He had done some tricks with the smoke from the nargile which had been entertaining.

This time, as soon as I entered the place, his face lit up and I was welcomed like the long lost cobber and he recounted the story of a year ago. The owner of the bar was there as well, and though last time he was fairly formal, this time he sat and chatted for a long time...mostly in English. I had a book...essential in a bar when you’re sitting alone and don’t want to appear a dork by looking at your phone...and he noted that last time I was there I had a book too. Did I like reading? Surprised? He’s a Turk...named Errol. Who would have thought it! Here we are...

My interaction with Uber re the massive overcharging mentioned in the last letter has had a not-totally-satisfactory ending. Yep, they’ll give me a credit for the overcharging, but it is only usable in...wait for it...India! Their system doesn’t allow changes in currency. For some reason I had to pay in cash in India, not by card, so therein lies the problem I suspect. I remonstrated crisply, but each reply was largely composed of cut-and-paste platitudes like ‘we understand you had a bad experience’ or ‘thank you for the feedback, we will use it to improve the Uber experience’ or ‘thankyou for your understanding’. I did beg that they cut the ‘thoughts-and-prayers’ insincerity and answer the questions I was asking, and finally they did. But still, 20 bucks of my money is waiting for me next time I’m in the subcontinent, so it’s not all bad.

I was in Madrid only for a couple of days, catching my breath before catching a ride south to Sevilla to start the Spanish course. I had thought of going by high speed train (300km/hr, takes 2 and a bit hours) but the much cheaper option of going by ride sharing won the day, even though it took 5 hours!

The ride-sharing app is called Blablacar. Online you put in where you want to go and then you get lined up with someone who is driving that way. I used it last year when I was here, and in all but one case it was fine. The one case involved my being at the Plaza de Toros in Granada at 6 am for the long ride to the coast to catch the ferry to Morocco. I had already paid the non refundable booking for accommodation so all seemed ready to roll. But the ride never turned up. I could have been at the wrong place, but the driver never answered my texts or calls as I stood in the drizzle and cold. So I jettisoned that part of the trip (there was no direct train or bus). They, Blablacar, did refund my money though.

This time there was no trauma. The driver picked me up on time, at the agreed rendezvous, and for the 550 km trip through green and luscious countryside I chatted with one of the other passengers, a youngish woman on her way to an interview for a position teaching psychology at a university. She had English, so I could chat in Spanish knowing that when incomprehension struck I could lapse into the mother tongue. The other passenger sat in the front with the driver. There was a freeway all the way, and the speeds were between 120 and 140 km/hr.

The airbnb is such a change from the last one I was in. The youngish bloke, Domenico, whose place it is, has good English, has two rooms for rent, and has a 3yo son, Maxi. (I don’t know the domestic arrangements there, but I dare say that if it’s any of my business I’ll find out). I felt immediately at home, did a load of washing, got some food from the supermarket, opened some wine and ate while he bathed the boy and watched tv. It feels really good.

On Monday I head to the school for a group activity/walk around the city, then start the one-on-one lessons at 2 pm. Two hours later, with what I imagine will be an overwrought brain, that’ll be that for the day. The weather is superb, cool air, warm sun, no clouds.

So...for what happens next, wait for the next letter.

Hasta luego (see you later)

T
