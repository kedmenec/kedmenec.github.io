Hi,

Today, Thursday 19th, is my last day in Turkey. Next stop is Kuala Lumpur, and after six days there I’m back in Oz.

Istanbul is in the throes of the annual tulip festival. The public gardens and any available space is stocked with nearly all colours, and grape hyacinths round out the palette. There is a tulip carpet on display near the Aya Sofya museum, the biggest in the world, or so the banner says. It is pretty big. Those are people at the other end.

*(photo missing: F28C16DD-A2DF-4E94-B1CD-DFB071569158-768x1024.jpeg)*

A big public garden called Gülhane Bahçesi, just below the Topkapı Palace, has vast beds of them as well.

The two big architectural attractions in the Sultanahmet square are the Aya Sofya museum at one end...

*(photo missing: 2DFA7100-DE73-4D73-9788-58C98C6959C7-768x1024.jpeg)*

And the Sultanahmet mosque at the other...

*(photo missing: FC5CC4F8-ADDD-4078-8B56-DCFA0D99A33F-768x1024.jpeg)*

These are both amazing structures, the first built in 537 CE and for a thousand years was the biggest church in the universe. The interior is amazing...

*(photo missing: F6C916A7-C41B-418D-8716-6B1DBDD73008-768x1024.jpeg)*

When Constantinople fell to the 24 year old Mehmet the Conqueror in 1453 the place became a mosque. Later, when Turkey became a republic in early 20th century, it became a museum.

The Sultanahmet mosque was finished in 1616 and its interior is equally gobsmacking.

*(photo missing: A88BC007-E938-4DC9-BF28-0F7A295F641D-768x1024.jpeg)*

*(photo missing: 3675F0B7-52A6-4170-A185-8B1BA25FB786-768x1024.jpeg)*

Occasionally in the street I saw youngish men with bandages on the back and front of their heads. Sometimes the bandages weren’t there, and bloody scabby skin was exposed. Here’s one from behind.

*(photo missing: 73BE1A07-5D6D-4AE7-8B51-F7C9C37F32CA-767x1024.jpeg)*

So what’s the story?

It seems that in Istanbul hair transplant operations are relatively cheap, so the not-wanting-to-be-balding chaps from other Mediterranean places, as well as locals, get the procedure done here. It looks immensely painful.

I had dinner again with Eray and we ended up at the Tezgha bar again (the one with the books and cool jazz). It was a good time again.

*(photo missing: E2848C52-3B0F-476C-B989-697545EF2162-1024x576.jpeg)*

I had the last breakfast at the usual cafe near the Grand Bazaar. I get addressed as Terry abi (meaning big brother Terry) by the waiters. Here they are having breakfast...

*(photo missing: 3E496966-C1E1-4BD1-B72E-4E1629FA03E9-1024x768.jpeg)*

Another young guy who was there last time, but has since moved on, came from Tajikistan, and my efforts to pronounce his name have cured my catarrh for ever. They are very welcoming and hospitable. The bloke on the right is named Cem ( pronounced Jem). He’s the boss, and was a career soldier before retirement. I think he may run the place a bit like the army...the forks and spoons line up for inspection on the table. But he’s very friendly.

The next letter will be from Koala Lumper...at the other end of 12 hours in the air. I haven’t got on the plane yet, but I am over it already.

All the best

T
