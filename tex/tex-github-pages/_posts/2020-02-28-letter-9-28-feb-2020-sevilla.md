Life in Sevilla has been splendid. The airbnb is perfect. The guy who owns it is charming, I feel exactly at home, his child is entertaining, the neighbourhood is quiet, I only have to walk 30 minutes to get to the school, the weather is magnificent...where do I stop?

In this first week at the school I have had 2 hr lessons in the afternoon with a teacher named Rocio. She’s a fairly severe looking woman of maybe 40 years, along with whom I get well. (See what learning another language does to the way you speak). I write a page of stuff about whatever for homework, and we go through it together, then we have a topic to chat about..travel, prejudice ...whatever. It has been great, and I am heartened by my developing abilities. I still find difficulty in understanding everything that she says, because there are so many extraneous sounds that I don’t expect...all the c sounds and z before an e or an i come out as th, and d and t are pretty much indistinguishable from a mushy sort of th too, so there’s a lot of lisp with only a few consonants to give meaning. But I’m improving. It will hit the fan when I attempt to talk with someone in the street. Sevillanas, ie people who live in Sevilla, are well known for speaking quickly, but that is an advantage, according to the teacher, because everything is slower everywhere else. Always look on the bright side!

Next week I change teacher. Rocio confided that as lots of enrolments have been cancelled because of the virus, the part time teachers have been rationalised as well, and she has been ‘let go’. Ah well, change is good.

I see a lot...well a few...people smoking in the street, and there are always some cigarette butts on the otherwise extremely clean streets. This widespread smoking, especially among the older types, could account for why complexions and voices are often gravelly, and that’s not just the women. Smoking inside is forbidden, so it is outside that the habit is expressed. There is a bit of a fashion for heavy makeup during the day, and some middle aged women are painted up as though they are going to something in the evening, but even the skilled use of the palette knife doesn’t always disguise the deep crevasses.

There is quite a bit of busking in the streets...here a group of ladies were singing some roistering songs with guitar and drum...

I prepare and eat my meals at the airbnb, which is much more economical, especially with the wine. For $6 one can buy a really nice red, and I have been taking advantage of that. Where in Australia would you drink a $6 red and retain the enamel on the teeth? The wineglass that’s on offer is huge...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/DDD335E3-CC66-43FE-8700-E4AECBE49B5D.jpeg' | relative_url }})

...so it’s a bit like drinking from a fishbowl, and it would be easy for one to end up drinking like a fish as well.

Spring has sprung here already (the locals all talk about the havoc that climate change has wreaked on the usual order of things) and the orange trees that one encounters when one least expects it, are laden with flowers.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2020/02/0187E0B2-E64B-4240-B10B-C2C96E3379D4.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It is intoxicating to round a corner and get walloped with a face full of such delicious aroma. The oranges are, appropriately, Sevilles...those nonsweet jobs that are used for marmalade. Funnily enough that’s what they are used for here too. One of the teachers who took a few of us on a walking tour remarked that Spaniards in general don’t eat marmalade all that often, so it gets exported to marmaladephilic countries like the UK.

The south of the country was part of an Islamic empire for more than 500 years, until Ferdinand and Isabella, the Catholic kings, took control in 1490, and then expelled every Muslim they could find, and for good measure deported as many Jews as they could as well. So around here there are so many influences remaining from that time....buildings, cooking styles, words ...those starting with ‘al’ are a giveaway as that means ‘the’ in Arabic. So we have algebra, alcohol, algorithm, Allah (the god), all from Arabic. In Spanish alfombra is a rug, almuerzo is lunch, almohad is pillow...and so on.
