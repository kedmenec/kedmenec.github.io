Hi,

Well folks, this is the last one for this trip.

I’m back in Kuala Lumpur after a couple of days in Sabah, the Malaysian state on the island of Borneo. I went there to visit a longtime friend and his family.

Here’s Andika (the dad) Kamelia (the mum) and Al Khalifa (the two year old).

It was a great time there. The boy is delightful.

Here was a great meal of sardines, prawns, greens and rice. And guava juice.

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2018/04/45C9538F-3CBE-4740-A96D-AC4C09556FE7.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Among other things, they have a mini market in the village where they live. Recently the river flooded and they had 80 cm of water through the shop. They are just getting it back into working order. Then there was the earthquake which cracked the walls. Andi planted 700 banana trees on some land that his grandfather-in-law owns, and despite having strengthened the fences, arrived one morning to find that a herd of cows had broken in and eaten the tops off most. Life is hard.

These cows, owned by someone I gather, roam the roads, eating of the lush vegetation, wander on the highways, congregate on the laneways at night, and are a real hazard. Apparently the owner rounds them up and sells them when he feels like it, but takes no responsibility it seems. They are just part of the scenery, and everyone moves around them.

It is very humid and tropical in Sabah, with a thunderstorm most afternoons. So it is very green and lush, but the concrete that most buildings are made from seems to degenerate quickly, and there is a Somerset Maugham, Malaysian rubber plantation decadence about it. Sabah, it seems, is neglected by the politicians, so minor roads, especially, are in poor condition...except for the ones that lead to the swish houses of the political classes. It is quite a contrast to be driving past the paddies, where houses are concrete, or rusting iron, on a good road, but once the two story Taj Mahal is passed the potholes start.

It wouldn’t happen in Oz ...er...would it?

Here’s Mt Kinabalu from my hotel window...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2018/04/550FD79D-DA08-4212-90FA-EDDF8ADDBDBE.jpeg' | relative_url }})

So now I’m back in the hotel in the commercial area of Kuala Lumpur, home of the giant shopping malls and tourists, a stone’s throw from the huge Petronas Towers. The hotel is in a back street, behind a major nightlife and restaurant thoroughfare, and in the past it has been a pretty disgusting area of potholes, open drains, overflowing rubbish skips, and at night, rats: like something out of Blade Runner. But since I was last here there have been massive improvements, the roads have been repaired, the drains renovated, the sleazy hotel facades spruced up, and it’s quite approachable. The hotel is modern and comfortable. Here is the view from the front...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2018/04/31AEDA3D-0654-49DF-90C8-3859F73AC404.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It doesn’t look all that salubrious, but from ground level it’s fine. You can see the degenerating concrete, the split system air cons that are thankfully everywhere, and the blue flags which are of one of the parties contesting the election next month.

I’ve eaten at the Estana Curry House, a short stroll away, most nights, and the welcome from the waiters, who remembered me from last year! is always warm. The waiters are mostly Indians or Bangladeshis, with various levels of English, but always polite and attentive.

It’s open 24 hours, and in the evening the soccer from somewhere is always playing on the tv.

One waiter is always there when I go ...he works from 8am to 8 pm..his name is Bapi and comes from Kolkata.

These guys have the best smiles (not shown here).

I have been having the egg roti for breakfast there...crisp crepe with egg inside...with various spicy juices to enliven the palate.![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2018/04/1D36EB0B-853F-4863-8813-E61A1770EA25.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Tomorrow I get the plane to Melbourne and the show is over for another while.

All the best to you.

T
