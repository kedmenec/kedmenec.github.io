Hi everyone,

The fierce looking guy at the back has been there for a couple of years, and his usual expression, which would scare a small child at a hundred paces, transforms into a great smile when greets you. The other two are much less intimidating.

Getting to Istanbul was effortless, and thanks to a couple of Bollywood epics on the inflight entertainment, the time flew quickly as well. I stayed in an airbnb near the Blue Mosque in the centre of the old part of the city. There were two Slovakian guys staying here too, and they were good for a chat. The room was great, and the the heating splendid. This last feature was welcome and the max temp was around the 6-8 degrees. A bit of a change from the 32 degrees in KL.

Most of my time was spent catching up with people I know, having the ego stroked as the spontaneous smile of recognition greeted my arrival at the shop or restaurant.

I spent an afternoon and evening with a very good friend named Sertaç. He was my online tutor in Turkish a few years ago and we get on very well. He is marrying his totally gorgeous and smart Russian girlfriend Anna next month, but unfortunately I will be in Spain so cannot attend.

Here they are in a cafe two years ago...

Here we are at the end, drinking yoghurt and soda water (traditional post hamam beverage). I’m doing a Gandhi impersonation there.

So, after 11, charged with booze and unconcern, as Kenneth Slessor might have written (a strange reference there...

In the poem ‘Country Towns’ the last verse says

Country towns with your schooner bees,  
And locusts burnt in the pepper-trees,  
Drown me with syrups, arch your boughs,  
Find me a bench, and let me snore,  
Till, charged with ale and unconcern,  
I'll think it's noon at half-past four!

Thank you Mrs Elwood in year 8 English all those years ago, for letting/making us learn stuff that enriches us half a century later).

Well, back to the story...I got into a bus which took me to Taksim Square, 20 mins back on the way home, I got the funicular tram down the hill side, and for the final leg of the journey, well past midnight, in the pouring rain, was about to get into the final tram when the guy said ‘ no...finish’.

Bloody hell, I thought. So, for the next hour I sloshed through the puddles, following the tram track back to Sultanahmet and a warm and dry room. I did have an umbrella. However, within sight of the destination, my shoe slipped on a slippery cobblestone and... luckily the ground broke my fall, ripped a lump of skin from my knee and tore a hole in my troosers. Ah, what a night.

The next day, when the blokes in my usual breakfast cafe saw the leaking blood on my next pair of trousers, they got all solicitous, one dashing off to the pharmacy to get some bandaids, the other wiping the excess fluids off and the third applying the dressing. I do love this place.

I got lots of welcomes in the Grand Bazaar too, some of whom I recognised from previous visits, others were less burnt into my memory. But I drank tea with all of them, and resisted buying anything!

On Sunday I got the flight to Tunisia. The Istanbul Ataturk International Airport is soon to be replaced by a brand spanking new one, and the inadequacy of the old one, in the face of millions more travellers was all too apparent. The queue to get to passport control was at least a hundred metres long, and tended to move slowly because of shameless queue jumpers. I admonished one middle aged couple, and explained that the end of the line was 50m back, and received some appreciative looks from other queuers. Woo woo, what a warrior for travelling ethics!

The plane was an hour late taking off. I did have some concerns when the cabin crew were just a bit slack in enforcing the mobiles off and seats in the upright position rules (one could hear the pings of incoming calls as we were taxiing) and then...15 seconds into the takeoff as the jet was gathering speed to leap into the air, suddenly the pilot hit the brakes and the giant sardine can, to the sound of gasps and screams, slewed to a grinding halt. Silence for a few seconds, then we trundled slowly to the next available U turn, went back along the adjacent runway, stopped and started again, and finally came to rest for a quarter of an hour or so: no announcements, no anxiety soothing explanations, nothing to prevent outraged travelers venting their fury on Facebook...nothing.

Then we started up again, got back onto the racetrack and off we went. The landing was pretty rough, but the crowd still clapped, and as we taxied in to the terminal many people got up and started to get their bags from the overhead lockers, while the stewards dashed about, enforcing the rules, and making them sit down.

This is truly another part of the universe.

So now I’m in Tunis, the capital of Tunisia, in a very basic Airbnb, right next to the main drag. The explorations start tomorrow.

See you again soon, with more pics than this time...I promise
