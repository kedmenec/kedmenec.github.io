Hi everyone,

I’ve had my week and a bit in Madrid, and tomorrow I get taken by a very fast train to Córdoba...it’s nearly 300km, and takes an hour and three quarters. Looks like it will be twice as fast as Vline from Kerang to Melbourne.

The weather here has been superb, high teens to low twenties, crisp in the morning, little or no wind, sunny.

I’ve been to the usual palaces, galleries, museums and enjoyed the experience. The most recent one was the Cerralbo Museum, a stately 17th century mansion, right near the royal palace and the cathedral, and now a place showing what filthy rich nobility lived like a century or so ago. The guy who owned the place was a mad collector, so the walls of the rooms are covered with old masters and bits of armour and ...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/D4CAAF1F-EB20-4DCA-96C8-B7C797AC3B8C.jpeg' | relative_url }})

The paintings were generally quite dark, and in the subdued lighting it was pretty hard to work out what was there. It made such a contrast with the paintings in the Prado museum, the big one here, where it seems everything has been renovated, and the years of cack cleaned off, leaving brilliant colours rather than Stygian gloom. I guess the Prado has a budget of millions, and the Cerralbo not.

Here’s the garden on one courtyard...

One day I went by bus to Toledo, an hour or so south of Madrid. The old city is an entanglement of winding streets, cobblestones and old buildings. The big attraction is the Cathedral, said to be the rival of St Peter’s in Rome, even though not as big. It is pretty mind blowing inside ...

That night I went out for eating and drinking with a ‘you must get in touch with X when you are in Madrid’ contact. It was a great evening of tapas and wine and lots of chat. Sadly I’m nowhere near good enough in spanish to be able to hold a conversation, but the new friend, Isabel Fernandez-Shaw, was more than fluent in English, so all was well. Her father was a Spanish ambassador, and she spent several years in Canberra, as well as many other parts of the world. Here we are after tapas (scallops in pastry, octopus, pudding, wine, grappa)...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/18143E36-EB7C-49DA-B37E-97C9EC7A0E63.jpeg' | relative_url }})

It was a ripper night.

There’s a lot to look at and listen to in the streets. One busker, a saxophonist, has been playing Jamaican Rumba, (with one interpolated extra beat which makes me grimace in a piano-teacherly way) every time I have walked by, but I’m sure he knows other things because he has two CDs for sale. There are string quartets, barrel organs, trumpeters, Italianate tenors singing O Sole Mio and Funiculi Funicular. There is never a quiet moment.

There is another bit of street theatre involving black guys who are selling handbags and sunglasses. They have their wares laid out on 1mx1m squares of cloth, but with string attached to each corner, and when one spies a cop approaching (I surmise that what they are doing is illegal) they pull the strings, the wares are wrapped into a bundle,and they melt into the crowd. Here are some setting up...

![]({{ '/tex/assets/uploads/2019/03/35E691A4-FF99-49B5-A4F6-CF2D2AFF873F.jpeg' | relative_url }})

They laugh a lot, but I imagine that life isn’t easy.

I went to a flamenco concert, just me and 35 others in a small room and a guitarist, a singer and two dancers. It was pretty amazing. How the singer kept his voice with such a strangled sound production is surprising...I guess there is technique to that style as well. The two women dancers were fascinating...such a range of facial expressions, tapping, swirling, twirling...I’m reminded of another poem Mrs Elwood had us learn in 1962 which describes it all so well...

So I’m off to Córdoba tomorrow. For most of my life I’ve thought of it as Cored-over, but now that I’m in the know I say CORdoba. There’s an accent on the o which says...er...accent the cor, and they spell it with a b. In Spanish the sound of b and v and pretty much the same, ie the same as a b. So there you have it.

Goodvye until next time

Terence
