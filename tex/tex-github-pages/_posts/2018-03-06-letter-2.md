I’m in Izmir now, and am about to start the second day of the language course. The plan is three hours of one on one conversation each day, which is the same deal as last year, and with the same teacher. So it feels like just a continuation of what ended a year ago ....er...which is what it is, except I can do it much better now.

The final few days in Istanbul were good. The weather was pretty awful, but the activities fine.

The night out with Buğrahan was a good one, and a friend of his named Osman came too, so it was quite jolly.

Then another night I had dinner with another friend from ten years ago, a software engineer, and great companion. We had some vegan food in a trendy area called Beyoğlu, then repaired for a couple of craft beers in a bar further up the Istiklal street. This joint is like your living room: big shelves of books, comfortable chairs, a bar at one end, cool jazz over the concealed stereo, just a few patrons and a laid back atmosphere verging on the catatonic. Together we solved the problems of the world. Here we are...

*(photo missing: second_trip_letter_march_-002-169x300.png)*

The flight from Istanbul to Izmir was only one hour, but the attendant sitting went from 10 am until the plane took off at 6pm.

Here’s the back story. Months ago I booked a flight leaving at 3.45 pm. But I got some reminder notices a couple of days before the flight, that it was at 1pm. Ok I thought, maybe a change has happened. (Actually a change had happened it seemed!). So I rocked up to the airport two hours early to be told that the 1pm flight didn’t exist, and I was on the 3.45pm. Hmm, strange, I thought. This company eDreams, which seemed to have taken over the administration of my booking, seems to have some systemic problems.

So I sat for an extra few hours, expecting to be on my way about 3.45. But...the plane was 2 hours late!!! I got to Izmir at 7pm, got the suburban train to my suburb, (that was another hour), and finally found the accommodation I was seeking. I wrote a series of complaints to eDreams, but got replies that suggested that I was somehow to blame for the situation. Well,that’s one fewer travel agents that I will consider in future.

The accomodation is in a flat with a 35 yo woman named Maral and her 7yo son Timur. It is very relaxed, she speaks some English and it’s fine. I played a PlayStation game with the boy for a while, and realised that the manual dexterity required to compete successfully in an army massacre simulation, with a 7yo is a bit beyond me. He chats away to me, but my compromised hearing means that a voice in the frequencies usually associated with a bat, as well as it all happening in a foreign language, is largely unintelligible.

I had lunch on Monday in the favourite fish restaurant from last year. The bear of a manager welcomed me like an old customer, and I had the favourite, sardines.

*(photo missing: second_trip_letter_march_-003-300x225.png)*

All that cost about $5, and was delish.

Today I lunched at the other favourite place near the school...the Kardelen...er....Cafe I suppose. Kardelen means snowdrop, as in the flower, and apparently is a common name for a lunchtime kebab/soup sort of joint. As soon as I walked in the pizza maker guy dusted off his hands and greeted me and the waiter gave me the old kiss kiss and called me by name. I had mercimek, ie lentil, soup to feed the stomach, but the reception fed the soul.
